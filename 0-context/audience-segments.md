# Audience segments

The reconciled target audience for AIOS events and campaigns — combines the stated
minimum bar for the Aug 2026 Ubud event with the broader segments John described.
Use this doc for application-question design and for calibrating any event/campaign
copy; see `personas/anonymized-archetypes.md` for the decision-making modes that cut
across these segments.

## The bar (applies to the Ubud event specifically)

An applicant needs to fit **at least one** of the segments below. The bar exists
because the session works best hands-on and small — it's a quality filter, not
gatekeeping for its own sake.

## Segments

### 1. Technical team (primary)

**Who:** Part of a small technical team already using tools like Slack and GitHub day
to day — as small as 2-3 people. Doesn't need to be a formal "engineering org," just
already working in a way AIOS's spine and harnesses can plug into.

**What draws them:** Concrete mechanism — a real workspace they can set up and use
that day, not a concept. They're peers evaluating a tool, not a stakeholder needing to
be sold.

**What moves them:** Show, don't pitch. Terminal commands, the actual folder spine,
what changes in their daily workflow. Skip persuasion — get to "here's what you'll
walk away running."

### 2. Company owner going all-in

**Who:** Runs a company (roughly 50-150 people) and wants to move the *whole*
organization forward with AI, not bolt on a few tools at the edges.

**What draws them:** Whole-org ambition, proof of real capability over a slide deck.
Maps closely to the founder-visionary archetype — see
`personas/anonymized-archetypes.md`.

**What moves them:** Proof of work, diagnostic accuracy, and an explicit "your people
run this after we leave" framing. Never imply ongoing dependency on outside help.
Anticipate that they'll need to justify the decision internally to a more skeptical
operational counterpart afterward — give them something concrete to bring back.

### 3. AI-adjacent service partner

**Who:** Delivers AI-related services themselves (consulting, training, tooling,
implementation) and isn't the direct buyer — but sits close to people who are, and
could refer a fit.

**What draws them:** Understanding AIOS well enough to know who to send, and a
credible signal that this isn't a competitor to what they do.

**What moves them:** Treat as a peer/referral relationship, not a lead. Be explicit
about what AIOS is and isn't so they can position it accurately to their own network.

### 4. Broader / self-identified fit

**Who:** Doesn't cleanly fit segments 1-3, but is seriously diving into AI (John's
open-ended "tech-forward founder," "AI studio founder building systems," etc.) and
believes they'd get real value from a hands-on afternoon.

**What draws them:** The chance to actually set something up, not another talk.

**What moves them:** Same tone-of-voice defaults as everyone else — concrete,
honest, no hype. Application review is where this segment gets calibrated case by
case; the form should invite self-identification here rather than screening it out.

## How this maps to the application form

Segments 1-3 map directly to a multiple-choice self-ID question; segment 4 is the
"other, tell us why" option. See
`2-work/campaigns/2026-08-05-aios-onboarding-ubud/application-questions.md`.
